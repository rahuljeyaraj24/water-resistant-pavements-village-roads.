# Water-Resistant Pavements for Village Roads

**Project type:** Civil engineering prototype / experimental study  
**Theme:** Affordable and durable rural infrastructure  
**Status:** Proposed prototype — results must be verified by laboratory testing.

## 1. Problem statement

Village roads and pathways are exposed to rainfall, moisture, water stagnation and repeated vehicle movement. These conditions can contribute to surface deterioration, cracking, reduced durability and recurring maintenance costs. Poor drainage and unsuitable construction practices can make the problem worse.

This project investigates whether a suitable hydrophobic concrete admixture can reduce water absorption while maintaining the strength and performance required for a pavement application.

## 2. Proposed solution

Prepare comparable concrete specimens:
- **Control mix:** concrete without the hydrophobic admixture.
- **Modified mix:** the same base mix with a compatible hydrophobic admixture at a manufacturer-recommended dosage.

Cure specimens under the same conditions and compare water absorption, compressive strength and visible condition using relevant standards and qualified supervision. Select a mix only after reviewing measured results, cost and constructability.

> **Important:** Water resistance does not replace good drainage, suitable pavement design, correct curing or quality control. An admixture may affect workability or strength. Do not claim improvement until tests confirm it.

## 3. Objectives

1. Study moisture-related deterioration in village-road pavements.
2. Compare control and hydrophobic-admixture concrete specimens.
3. Measure water absorption and compressive strength using appropriate laboratory procedures.
4. Record material quantities, dosage, curing conditions, test age and results.
5. Estimate material cost and discuss practical rural-road applications.

## 4. Methodology

1. Review relevant concrete and pavement standards with a faculty guide.
2. Select materials and confirm admixture compatibility with the supplier.
3. Fix a base mix design appropriate to the intended application; do not assume a mix is pavement-ready without design checks.
4. Prepare replicated control and modified specimens, documenting exact quantities.
5. Cure specimens under comparable conditions.
6. Test water absorption and compressive strength using applicable standards and laboratory equipment.
7. Compare results, variability and cost. Report both positive and negative findings.
8. Recommend field trials only if laboratory performance and engineering review support them.

## 6. How to use the sample data

`data/sample-data.csv` is a **blank data-collection template with one clearly marked illustrative row**. The illustrative values are not experimental evidence and must not be reported as measured results. Replace them with actual laboratory readings before analysis or submission.

## 7. Safety and quality

- Work under a qualified faculty/laboratory supervisor.
- Follow product safety data sheets (SDS), PPE requirements and laboratory procedures.
- Use the admixture dosage and mixing sequence recommended by the manufacturer.
- Do not conduct full-scale road construction without engineering approval, mix design, drainage review and applicable local permissions.
- Use appropriate standards and report the standard/version followed.

## 8. Expected outcome

The project will provide a documented comparison of water absorption, compressive strength, observed condition and estimated cost for the tested mixes. Whether the modified mix performs better is an experimental question; conclusions must be based on actual results.

## 9. Suggested references to consult

Consult the latest applicable editions available through your institution or standards library, including relevant BIS/IS standards for concrete mix proportioning, compressive-strength testing, water absorption/durability testing and pavement design. Confirm the exact method and edition with your faculty guide before testing.
