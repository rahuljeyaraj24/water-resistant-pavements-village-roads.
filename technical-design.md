# Technical Design Document

## 1. Overview

This project is a controlled laboratory comparison of conventional concrete and concrete containing a compatible hydrophobic admixture. The purpose is to assess moisture resistance without compromising strength or other requirements for the intended pavement.

## 2. Conceptual architecture

Materials selection → mix design review → specimen preparation → controlled curing → laboratory tests → data recording → comparison → engineering recommendation.

See `architecture.svg` for a visual workflow.

## 3. Inputs

- Cement and aggregates meeting the selected mix-design requirements
- Water suitable for concrete production
- Hydrophobic admixture with manufacturer technical data sheet and SDS
- Approved mix design and documented dosage
- Moulds, mixing equipment, curing facilities and calibrated test equipment
- Test procedures and supervision approved by the institution

## 4. Experimental groups

### Group A — Control
Base concrete mix without hydrophobic admixture.

### Group B — Modified
Same base mix with the selected compatible hydrophobic admixture, dosed according to the manufacturer's guidance and approved mix-design procedure.

Keep aggregate grading, water-cement ratio, batching, compaction, curing and test age comparable. Record any unavoidable differences.

## 5. Measurements

| Measurement | Record |
|---|---|
| Batch details | Date, mix ID, material source and batch number |
| Admixture | Product, dosage, unit and supplier recommendation |
| Fresh concrete | Workability/consistency observation and relevant test result |
| Curing | Start/end dates and curing conditions |
| Water absorption | Method/standard, specimen ID, masses and calculated result |
| Compressive strength | Test age, specimen dimensions, failure load and calculated strength |
| Cost | Material quantity, unit price, cost per batch and cost per unit volume |
| Observations | Cracks, surface defects, unusual behavior and deviations |

Use the applicable laboratory standard for specimen dimensions, conditioning, calculations and reporting. Do not mix results from different methods as if they were directly comparable.

## 6. Evaluation approach

Compare group averages and variation only after enough replicate specimens have been tested. Report sample count and all exclusions. Calculate percentage difference only when the reference value is non-zero and the comparison is methodologically valid.

A lower water-absorption result alone is not sufficient to approve a pavement mix. Strength, workability, abrasion/freeze-thaw or other exposure requirements as applicable, shrinkage, cost, drainage and construction feasibility also matter.

## 7. Acceptance and decision criteria

Set acceptance criteria with the faculty guide before testing. Use applicable codes, project requirements and the admixture manufacturer's documented limitations. Do not invent pass/fail thresholds after seeing results.

## 8. Risks and controls

- **Strength reduction:** test compressive strength and other required properties.
- **Poor compatibility/workability:** conduct trial batches and document fresh-concrete behavior.
- **Incorrect dosage:** follow the current product data sheet and weigh materials accurately.
- **Misleading results:** use replicates, consistent procedures and calibrated equipment.
- **Water ponding:** maintain adequate road crossfall, drainage and surface design; water repellency is not a drainage system.
- **Field-use risk:** do not recommend construction without qualified engineering review and applicable approvals.

## 9. Deliverables

- Material and mix records
- Raw test observations
- Calculations and comparison charts/tables
- Cost estimate with stated assumptions
- Limitations and recommendations based on evidence
- Photographs of actual specimens, labelled by mix ID and test date
